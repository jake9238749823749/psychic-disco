# Maturity Scale

Score each of the five dimensions 1–5. Be honest — this is for you, not for investors.

**1 — Accidental.** The dimension isn't managed at all. You couldn't answer the question without
going to look, and looking would scare you.

**2 — Aware.** You know the answer and it's bad. You've thought about it. Nothing structural is in place.

**3 — Controlled.** There's a mechanism — a cap, a check, a review, a measured number. It works when
people remember to run it. It doesn't run itself.

**4 — Systematic.** The mechanism runs on its own: scheduled, automated, or enforced by the harness.
A new person inheriting the system would get the same protection without being told.

**5 — Compounding.** Not only systematic — it improves by itself. Every incident tightens it, stale
controls get pruned, and the trend line over quarters is visibly up and to the right.

## Reading the total (out of 25)

- **20–25:** You have a system. Run this audit quarterly and keep the trend.
- **14–19:** You have an operation with structural gaps. The lowest-scoring dimension is your next project.
- **8–13:** You have a demo that survived contact with production. Pick the single lowest dimension and
  fix it before scaling anything.
- **7 or below:** Do not scale. Do not add agents. Your next 30 days are hardening, not building.

## The only rule

The lowest score is the audit. A 5 in compounding doesn't cancel a 1 in blast radius.
Fix the floor before polishing the ceiling.
